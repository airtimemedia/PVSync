Try the demo Paper BibTeX

PVSync finds how far the audio is off, corrects it, then checks every sound against the shape of the lips. Click a clip to open it in the demo.

TL;DRLip movements can match the timing of speech without matching the spoken sounds. PVSync is a unified model for audio–visual offset estimation and phoneme-level articulation scoring, trained with viseme labels derived automatically from forced-aligned transcripts. On videos from 13 talking-head video generation models it matches human rankings of lip-sync quality at Spearman 0.83, against 0.34 for LSE-C.

Inside the model

Sounds and lip shapes land in the same places

Every dot is one phone from held-out speech, embedded by PVSync. Colours are the paper's eight viseme classes: groups of sounds that look the same on the lips. Hover a dot to see its class; hover a class to light it up.

Why some classes mix

The lip-explicit classes, bilabial (/p b m/) and labiodental (/f v/), are recognised almost perfectly. The errors fall between classes that look alike on the lips:

  • Velar scatters over its neighbours. /k/ and /g/ have no lip gesture of their own, so the mouth during them shows the neighbouring vowel. It is the weakest class.
  • Open and spread vowels are exchanged: a vowel such as /æ/ (“cat”) opens the mouth only slightly more than a spread vowel such as /ɛ/ (“bed”).
  • Rhotic and rounded are exchanged, because /r/ is produced with rounded lips.

In the paper, 70% of the final model's errors fall on one of these look-alike classes.

Method

How PVSync works

A clip is scored in 12-frame (480 ms) windows. AV-HuBERT reads the mouth, wav2vec2 reads the audio, and two small heads project both into one shared space, where the score is their cosine similarity.

Stage 1

Learn timing

Both towers train contrastively on 12-frame windows: each video window's positive is its own audio, and its negatives are the audio of the other clips in the batch. This stage alone already recovers the offset more accurately than every baseline.

Stage 2

Learn lip shapes

Every phone's span is pooled in both towers, and a video span is pulled toward audio spans of the same viseme class from other clips. Spans from the same clip are excluded, so nothing but the viseme can satisfy the loss.

Stage 3

Recover the timing

Viseme training gives up a little timing. Freezing both encoders, which keeps the viseme clustering, and fine-tuning only the two projection heads for sync brings offset accuracy to 0.927, above stage 1.

Where the phone timings come from. Transcripts are force-aligned to the audio with the Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA), which gives every phone a start and end time. A phone's interval selects the tokens it covers in both towers; the same spans give each phone its score at inference. No manual labels are involved.
